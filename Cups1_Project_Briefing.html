In one paragraph

Cups1 will be a B2B company that sells custom-printed paper cups and food packaging to café groups, restaurants and chains, starting in the USA and Canada. We don't own a factory. We find and check factories in Asia, manage printing, quality and shipping, and sell under our own name at a delivered price with the compliance documents included. 02

Business model (final)

Cups1 is an asset-light B2B sourcing and trading company. It owns no factory and no warehouse. It runs two business types.

1. Trading company / reseller (main)2. Sourcing agent (side)
How we earnProduct margin: buy from the factory, sell under the Cups1 nameCommission: 3–10% of factory price, minimum about US$1,000 per order
Who pays whomBuyer pays Cups1 the full price; Cups1 pays the factoryBuyer pays the factory; Cups1 bills only its fee
DeliveryUSA & Canada: delivered, duties paid (DDP). Other regions: FOB or CIF firstBuyer imports
Who carries the riskCups1 (quality, delays, payment)Mostly the buyer
Typical buyerCafé groups (3–30 stores), restaurants, chains, caterersDistributors and wholesalers
Supplier nameHidden (Cups1 documents, neutral cartons)Disclosed

Plus service fees: design, Sample Kit, quality inspection, retainers. Not a manufacturer, warehouse distributor, marketplace or dropshipper.

How an order runs

Requirement→RFQ to 2–3 factories→Cups1 quote→Deposit→Artwork proof→Production→Inspection→Balance→Ship & documents→Reorder

Money rules (fixed)

  • Never pay a factory before the buyer has paid us at least the same amount.
  • First orders with any factory go through Alibaba Trade Assurance.
  • Factory balance only after the buyer's balance arrives and inspection passes.
  • Shipping documents released only after full payment.
03

Products and market

What we sell

Disposable paper food packaging, mostly printed with the buyer's logo. Hero product: 12oz double-wall hot cup.

  • Double-wall hot cups 8 / 12 / 16 oz
  • Single-wall hot cups 4–20 oz
  • Ripple-wall cups 8 / 12 / 16 oz
  • Ice-cream and dessert cups with lids
  • Kraft salad and food bowls
  • Takeaway boxes (lunch, bakery window, pizza)

Who buys and where

  • Launch market: USA & Canada (worldwide later)
  • Sweet spot: 30,000–100,000 cups per order
  • First target: café group owners with 3–30 stores
  • Then: procurement managers at chains, universities, caterers
  • Then: regional distributors (sourcing-agent service)

Why this gap: digital printers are too expensive at 30k+ cups; factories are too hard to buy from directly.

Rule by regionWhat applies
USAFood contact: FDA 21 CFR 176.170 test report. State PFAS laws (e.g. CA, NY, WA, MN, CO): PFAS-free test report. "Compostable" only for BPI-certified items. FTC Green Guides on eco claims.
CanadaCBSA anti-dumping and anti-subsidy case on Chinese paper cups (estimated margins 75.8% + 49.6%); preliminary decision due by 2026-11-16. Use Vietnam-made cups. Competition Act requires proof for green claims.
Other regions (later)EU: 10/2011, LFGB, Single-Use Plastics Directive. Check each country before quoting delivered prices.

Why Vietnam matters

US duty on paper cups (HTS 4823.69.00): ≈ 37.5% from China vs ≈ 12.5% from Vietnam. Canada's case could add very high duties on Chinese cups. Rates changed several times in 2026; a customs broker re-checks before each quote.

  • Protect against factories going direct: service, contracts, hidden supplier
08

What competitor websites teach us

14 of the 15 competitor sites were measured live (fonts, colours, buttons, structure), plus 6 earlier reference sites.

Findings

  • 12 of 15 lead with "get a quote", not a cart
  • Only 3 show minimum and lead time up front, and those are the clearest sites
  • Only 4 mention certificates; none shows a real document
  • Closest rivals use generic templates and stock-style photos

Four gaps we can own

  • A Documents page with real (redacted) test reports
  • A delivered-price example: product + freight + duty
  • A sample inspection report
  • Plain-language tariffs and origin page

Colour warning

Navy + orange is already used by Leeline Sourcing (almost identical), LogoCup and KimEcopak. We stand out through the paper background, orange only on buttons and prices, and real documents.

1 · Email outreach

  • Verify every address; find decision makers for C-grade leads on LinkedIn
  • Separate sending domain, SPF/DKIM/DMARC, 2–3 weeks warm-up
  • 20–30 new emails a day per inbox, plain text, one personal line
  • 4 emails over 3 weeks: intro + free logo mock-up → follow-up → PFAS paperwork → polite close
  • Postal address + opt-out in every email (CAN-SPAM, CASL)

2 · Ads

  • Google Search, $300: "custom paper cups", "coffee cups with logo", "custom bubble tea cups", "custom paper cups canada"
  • Negative keywords: free, DIY, party, wedding, Etsy…
  • Meta retargeting $60 + Bing $40 from week 6
  • LinkedIn ads only from month 3–4, if email and Google work
  • Goal: cost per quote request under $80

3 · SEO website

  • A page per search intent: hot cups, cold/bubble tea cups, bowls & boxes, Canada, compliance documents, Sample Kit
  • 2 articles a month (cup sizes, PFAS rules, MOQ, landed cost…)
  • Fast pages, schema, Search Console in week 3
  • Directory listings and coffee trade guest articles; no bought links

From lead to sale

Reply within 2 h→Logo mock-up in 24 h→15-min call→Sample Kit in 3 days→Delivered-price quote in 48 h→Follow up day 3 / 7 / 14→Won: deposit → proof → production → delivery → reorder reminder

Rules we never break

  • No claim we can't attach a document for (no unqualified "eco"; "compostable" only for certified items)
  • No bought email lists, no hidden unsubscribe, no fake urgency or reviews
  • No quote without a real supplier price behind it; never show the supplier's name
  • Spam complaints under 0.3% (aim under 0.1%); lawyer checks email and claims rules before launch
